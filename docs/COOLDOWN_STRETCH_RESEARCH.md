# Five-minute guided cool-down research

## Product decision

The guided cool-down is an optional, comfort-first transition after a workout. It uses slow breathing, gentle mobility, and an easy range. It does **not** claim to prevent injury, cure soreness, or replace clinical advice.

## Design rules

- Use local, existing Strength Level motion loops for two target-aware cards; this avoids a network download and keeps the visual language consistent.
- Each card asks for a comfortable 45-second block (or a slow one-minute march for Endurance), with sides alternated where relevant.
- Coach wording: move slowly, breathe normally, feel only mild tension, do not bounce or force range.
- Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, chest symptoms, numbness/tingling, or feeling unwell. People with injury, relevant conditions, or pregnancy should use an appropriate clinician-approved modification.
- Reduced Motion shows a still Strength Level frame instead of an animated loop.

## Evidence and limits

- The NHS provides a roughly five-minute post-exercise stretch routine, with gentle 15–20 second holds and a recommendation to adapt for injury, health conditions, pregnancy, or individual needs. [NHS: How to stretch after exercising](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-stretch-after-exercising/)
- ACSM advises active low-intensity cooldown before mobility and recommends stretching at a mild sensation—not sharp/intense pain—after heart rate settles. [ACSM: Road map to effective muscle recovery](https://www.acsm.org/docs/default-source/files-for-resource-library/a-road-map-to-effective-muscle-recovery.pdf)
- A systematic review found insufficient evidence that post-exercise stretching materially improves strength recovery or DOMS. The app therefore frames this feature as optional comfort and mobility, not a recovery guarantee. [Afonso et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34025459/)
- A later meta-analysis supports static stretching for flexibility, while dose/individual needs vary. [Hosseini et al., 2024/25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39614059/)
